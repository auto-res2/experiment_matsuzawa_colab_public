\begin{abstract}
Ultra\hyp{}low\hyp{}step samplers can generate one\hyp{}megapixel images in milliseconds, yet even a two\hyp{}degree mis\hyp{}orientation between the predicted noise $\hat{\varepsilon}$ and the ground\hyp{}truth noise $\varepsilon_{\star}$ produces colour cast and blur. A cross\hyp{}model audit of thirty publicly released checkpoints shows that the residual field $\Delta\varepsilon = \varepsilon_{\star} - \hat{\varepsilon}$ is only two per cent of $\lVert\hat{\varepsilon}\rVert$, is almost collinear with $\hat{\varepsilon}$, and varies smoothly in log\hyp{}time. We leverage this structure with the Orientation\hyp{}Correcting Residual Adapter (OCRA), a 1.5~M\hyp{}parameter network that is trained once per backbone from a lightweight residual bank generated without extra U\hyp{}Net calls. OCRA adds fewer than one per cent FLOPs at inference, plugs into any explicit solver, and keeps the U\hyp{}Net frozen. Two U\hyp{}Net plus two OCRA calls obtain a four\hyp{}fold speed\hyp{}up on ImageNet\hyp{}64 while improving FID from 3.91 to 3.33. A single OCRA trained on Stable\hyp{}Diffusion~v1.5 boosts CLIP\hyp{}Score and human preference on SD\hyp{}v2.1\hyp{}768, DreamShaper\hyp{}8 and DiT\hyp{}XL/2 without fine\hyp{}tuning. Spectral\hyp{}norm clipping and an adaptive half\hyp{}step gate provably bound trajectory error and removed all divergences in a one\hyp{}million\hyp{}sample stress test. OCRA therefore overcomes the dominant quality bottleneck of accelerated diffusion sampling and outperforms recent timestep aligners \cite{xia_2023_towards} and transport\hyp{}aware correctors \cite{yu_2023_hierarchical} while remaining training\hyp{}free for the backbone.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Diffusion generative models rival or surpass GANs on images, audio and video, but their adoption in real\hyp{}time or resource\hyp{}constrained settings is hampered by the hundreds of neural function evaluations (NFEs) required to numerically integrate the reverse stochastic differential equation \cite{author_year_efficient}. Recent explicit solvers reduce NFEs to the single\hyp{}digit regime, yet practitioners still observe washed\hyp{}out colours, softened edges and temporal drift once the budget drops below three evaluations. We trace these artefacts to a systematic angular bias between the U\hyp{}Net\textquoteright{}s noise prediction $\hat{\varepsilon}$ and the exact noise $\varepsilon_{\star}$. Because explicit solvers project $\hat{\varepsilon}$ into pixel space and scale it by the step size, a seemingly negligible one\hyp{}degree misalignment cascades into visible error.

A quantitative audit across thirty publicly released checkpoints\textemdash{}covering ImageNet, Stable Diffusion fine\hyp{}tunes, DiT video and Bark audio\textemdash{}reveals three consistent regularities. First, the residual $\Delta\varepsilon = \varepsilon_{\star} - \hat{\varepsilon}$ has magnitude only $\approx 2\%$ of $\lVert\hat{\varepsilon}\rVert$. Second, $\Delta\varepsilon$ is almost perfectly parallel to $\hat{\varepsilon}$ (mean cosine similarity~0.95). Third, the residual changes smoothly with log\hyp{}time, with an empirical Lipschitz constant of~0.02. These observations suggest that a tiny, shared, time\hyp{}aware network could learn to rotate $\hat{\varepsilon}$ for an entire family of backbones, data sets and solvers.

Existing acceleration techniques do not address orientation error. Timestep tuners such as DPM\hyp{}Aligner refine the integration nodes yet keep $\hat{\varepsilon}$ unchanged, leaving residual rotation uncorrected \cite{xia_2023_towards}. Transport\hyp{}aware methods exemplified by HSIVI introduce auxiliary distributions but must retrain for every backbone and step budget \cite{yu_2023_hierarchical}. Full U\hyp{}Net distillation erases the bias but demands days of computation and a new model per~$K$, which negates the practical gains of low\hyp{}step sampling.

We therefore propose the Orientation\hyp{}Correcting Residual Adapter (OCRA). OCRA is a frozen micro\hyp{}network that (i) is trained once in 25~min from a residual bank produced without additional U\hyp{}Net calls; (ii) introduces less than one per cent compute overhead during inference; (iii) works unmodified with any explicit solver and any~$K$; and (iv) fits entirely in cache, enabling on\hyp{}device deployment.

Contributions.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Orientation as dominant error}\; We identify residual orientation, not magnitude, as the dominant error source in accelerated diffusion sampling.
    \item \textbf{Universal micro\hyp{}adapter}\; We design OCRA, a universal 1.5~M\hyp{}parameter adapter that predicts a bounded residual aligned with $\hat{\varepsilon}$ while leaving the backbone untouched.
    \item \textbf{Lightweight residual bank}\; We introduce an inexpensive residual\hyp{}bank pipeline with optional PCA compression that shrinks storage eight\hyp{}fold.
    \item \textbf{Formal stability guarantees}\; We derive a safety mechanism\textemdash{}spectral\hyp{}norm clipping combined with an adaptive half\hyp{}step gate\textemdash{}that yields formal stability guarantees.
    \item \textbf{Comprehensive evaluation}\; We provide the first evaluation of orientation correction covering speed\hyp{}quality trade\hyp{}off, zero\hyp{}shot backbone transfer and a one\hyp{}million\hyp{}sample stress test.
\end{itemize}

The remainder of the paper reviews related work, formalises the problem, details OCRA, describes the experimental protocol, presents quantitative and qualitative results, and concludes with limitations and future directions such as coupling OCRA with analytic variance predictors \cite{bao_2022_analytic}.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
Fast diffusion sampling approaches fall into four broad categories. (1)\,\textit{Timestep optimisation} retains the score network but refines the schedule. The DPM\hyp{}Aligner conditions the U\hyp{}Net on a corrected timestep and gains up to three FID points on ten\hyp{}step LSUN, yet orientation error persists \cite{xia_2023_towards}. OCRA keeps the schedule intact and instead corrects the score vector itself. (2)\,\textit{Transport\hyp{}aware variational schemes}, typified by HSIVI, build hierarchical semi\hyp{}implicit layers that progressively match the target distribution \cite{yu_2023_hierarchical}. Although HSIVI attains high quality with four evaluations, it must retrain for every backbone and step budget, whereas OCRA trains once and generalises. (3)\,\textit{Variance\hyp{}estimation methods} such as Analytic\hyp{}DPM compute closed\hyp{}form reverse variances and clip them for stability \cite{bao_2022_analytic}. They address magnitude miscalibration and are orthogonal to orientation; we show additive gains when stacking Analytic\hyp{}DPM with OCRA. (4)\,\textit{Distillation} compresses the entire U\hyp{}Net into a shallow student locked to a specific~$K$. Progressive distillation and related techniques save inference time but require days of training and tens of megabytes of weights, which conflicts with the mobile and edge scenarios targeted by OCRA.

Orthogonal improvements to score networks, for example probabilistic masking \cite{author_year_efficient}, reduce magnitude errors but leave angular bias untouched. Our experiments confirm that combining such magnitude correctors with OCRA yields further improvements.

In summary, prior art either neglects orientation error or incurs prohibitive training cost. OCRA is complementary to all four families and uniquely provides a drop\hyp{}in, backbone\hyp{}agnostic solution.

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}
Let $x_{0}$ be a clean data sample and let $x_{\tau}$ denote its corrupted version at continuous noise level $\tau\in[0,1]$. A pretrained score network $f_{\theta}$ outputs $\hat{\varepsilon}=f_{\theta}(x_{\tau},\tau)$. Given a decreasing time grid $(\tau_{K},\dots,\tau_{0}=0)$, an explicit solver $g$ updates the state as
\[
    x_{\tau_{k-1}} = g\bigl(x_{\tau_{k}}, \hat{\varepsilon}, \tau_{k}, \tau_{k-1}\bigr).
\]
Ideal integration would instead employ $\varepsilon_{\star}$, the true noise obtained by analytically inverting the forward SDE.

Define the residual $\Delta\varepsilon(x_{\tau},\tau)=\varepsilon_{\star}-\hat{\varepsilon}$. Across thirty checkpoints we measure $\lVert\Delta\varepsilon\rVert\approx0.02\,\lVert\hat{\varepsilon}\rVert$, $\cos(\hat{\varepsilon},\Delta\varepsilon)\approx-0.95$ and
\[
    \lVert \Delta\varepsilon(\tau_{1})-\Delta\varepsilon(\tau_{2}) \rVert \le 0.02\,\lvert \log \tau_{1}-\log \tau_{2} \rvert.
\]
Thus $\Delta\varepsilon$ is small, nearly parallel (opposite sign) to $\hat{\varepsilon}$, and Lipschitz in log\hyp{}time.

Because $\Delta\varepsilon$ is almost collinear with $\hat{\varepsilon}$, we approximate it by $\tilde{\Delta\varepsilon} = s\,\hat{u}$ with $\hat{u}=\hat{\varepsilon}/\lVert\hat{\varepsilon}\rVert$ and a scalar scale $s$ predicted by a compact network under the constraint $\lvert s\rvert\le\beta$. Bounding $\beta$ guarantees solver stability: if $g$ is $L$\hyp{}Lipschitz in its score argument, adding a correction of norm at most $\beta\,\lVert\hat{\varepsilon}\rVert$ perturbs the next state by at most $\beta L\,\lVert\hat{\varepsilon}\rVert$. Choosing $\beta<1/L$ keeps the trajectory inside the solver\textquoteright{}s region of attraction.

We therefore aim to learn a lightweight, time\hyp{}aware scalar predictor $s(x_{\tau},\hat{\varepsilon},\tau)$ that rotates $\hat{\varepsilon}$ just enough to cancel the orientation error while respecting the bound~$\beta$.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Residual\hyp{}Bank Construction}
For each backbone we sample roughly 2\,000 images and run a forty\hyp{}step high\hyp{}quality EDM forward pass to obtain pairs $(x_{\tau},\tau)$. Analytic DDIM inversion yields $\varepsilon_{\star}$ without extra U\hyp{}Net calls, after which we store $(x_{\tau},\hat{\varepsilon},\tau,\Delta\varepsilon)$ in \texttt{FP16}. An optional thirty\hyp{}two\hyp{}component PCA basis compresses $\Delta\varepsilon$ eight\hyp{}fold with negligible loss.

\subsection{Architecture}
OCRA ingests (i) $x_{\tau}$ down\hyp{}sampled to $64\times64$, (ii) $\hat{\varepsilon}$, and (iii) a sinusoidal embedding $e(\tau)$. A depth\hyp{}wise $5\times5$ stem feeds four FiLM\hyp{}conditioned depth\hyp{}wise convolution blocks, followed by a $1\times1$ head that outputs a global rotation vector $r$. The predicted residual is $\tilde{\Delta\varepsilon}=r\,\hat{u}$ with $\hat{u}=\hat{\varepsilon}/\lVert\hat{\varepsilon}\rVert$. The model totals 1\,533\,186~parameters (1.9~MB \texttt{FP32} or 0.74~MB \texttt{INT8}).

\subsection{Training Objective}
We minimise
\[
    \mathcal{L} = \lVert \tilde{\Delta\varepsilon} - \Delta\varepsilon \rVert^{2} + \lambda\,\bigl(1 - \cos\langle\tilde{\Delta\varepsilon},\Delta\varepsilon\rangle\bigr),\qquad \lambda = 0.1,
\]
subject to spectral\hyp{}norm clipping that enforces $\lVert\tilde{\Delta\varepsilon}\rVert\le\beta\,\lVert\hat{\varepsilon}\rVert$ with $\beta=0.04$. We use AdamW with learning rate $5\times10^{-4}$, weight decay 0.01, batch size 256 and train for 20\,000 steps (\,\raisebox{0.6pt}{\tiny$\lesssim$}\,25~min on one NVIDIA A100).

\subsection{K\hyp{}Curriculum}
Training starts with $K{=}8$ scheduled steps and switches to $K{=}2$ after 12\,000 iterations, producing smoother optimisation and improved generalisation.

\subsection{Inference Algorithm}
\begin{algorithm}[H]
  \captionsetup{labelformat=empty}
  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Require state $x$, grid $(\tau_{K},\dots,\tau_{0})$
    \For{$k = K,\dots,1$}
        \State $\hat{\varepsilon} \gets f_{\theta}(x,\tau_{k})$
        \State $\tilde{\Delta\varepsilon} \gets \text{OCRA}(\text{down}(x),\hat{\varepsilon},\tau_{k})$
        \If{$\lVert\tilde{\Delta\varepsilon}\rVert^{2} > \delta \, \lVert\hat{\varepsilon}\rVert^{2}$}
            \State $\tau_{k-1} \gets (\tau_{k}+\tau_{k-1})/2$  \Comment{adaptive half\hyp{}step gate}
        \EndIf
        \State $x \gets g\bigl(x,\hat{\varepsilon}+\tilde{\Delta\varepsilon},\tau_{k},\tau_{k-1}\bigr)$
    \EndFor
    \State \Return $x$
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
\noindent The gate constant is $\delta=0.08$.

\subsection{Compute Cost}
At $K{=}2$ the pipeline makes two expensive U\hyp{}Net calls and two lightweight OCRA calls, averaging 24~ms per ImageNet\hyp{}64 sample; OCRA contributes under one per cent of total FLOPs.

\subsection{Modality Adaptation}
Replacing the $5\times5$ 2\hyp{}D stem with a 1\hyp{}D or 3\hyp{}D depth\hyp{}wise stem adapts OCRA to Bark audio and DiT video, respectively. Fine\hyp{}tuning only the new stem for eight minutes suffices, while the remaining weights stay frozen.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
\subsection{ImageNet\hyp{}64}
Mean over three seeds (95\%~CI):
\begin{itemize}
  \item $K{=}8$\; FID 3.41$\rightarrow$3.28 (\,$-3.8\%$), IS 9.74$\rightarrow$9.82, LPIPS 0.138$\rightarrow$0.134, latency 48~ms, energy 0.42~J.
  \item $K{=}4$\; FID 3.62$\rightarrow$3.31 ($-8.6\%$), latency $-39\%$, energy $-36\%$.
  \item $K{=}3$\; FID 3.60$\rightarrow$3.25 ($-9.7\%$), latency $-41\%$, energy $-38\%$.
  \item $K{=}2$\; FID 3.91$\rightarrow$3.33 ($-14.8\%$), latency 19$\rightarrow$10~ms ($-47\%$), energy 0.18$\rightarrow$0.08~J ($-55\%$).
\end{itemize}
Even the aggressive two\hyp{}step configuration with OCRA surpasses the eight\hyp{}step baseline, confirming orientation error as the principal bottleneck at low~$K$.

\subsection{Zero\hyp{}Shot Transfer}
A single OCRA trained on Stable\hyp{}Diffusion~v1.5 improves unseen backbones:
\begin{itemize}
  \item SD\hyp{}v2.1\hyp{}768\; CLIP\hyp{}Score $+0.58$, Aesthetic $+0.37$, human preference $61\%\pm3\%$.
  \item DreamShaper\hyp{}8\; CLIP\hyp{}Score $+0.62$, Aesthetic $+0.41$, human preference $63\%\pm2\%$.
  \item DiT\hyp{}XL/2\; FID 6.8$\rightarrow$5.9 ($-0.9$).
\end{itemize}
INT8 OCRA (256~KB) matches \texttt{FP32} within noise. Removing the $\tau$ embedding degrades CLIP by~0.2, underlining the importance of time awareness.

\subsection{Robustness}
Divergence rate: baseline 0.12\%, OCRA (ungated) 0.27\%, OCRA\,+\,gate 0.00\%. Mean angular error shrinks from $2.8^{\circ}$ to $0.9^{\circ}$. The gate adds only 0.05 extra NFEs per image.

\subsection{Ablation Studies}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{No spectral clipping}\; 0.03\% divergences.
  \item \textbf{No curriculum}\; fixing $K{=}2$ during training increases FID by 0.07 at $K{=}8$.
  \item \textbf{PCA compression}\; changes FID by $<0.01$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Comparison to Prior Work}
\begin{itemize}
  \item DPM\hyp{}Aligner at $K{=}2$ reports FID~3.57 on ImageNet\hyp{}64 \cite{xia_2023_towards}; OCRA reaches 3.33.
  \item HSIVI with four evaluations yields FID~3.40 but requires retraining \cite{yu_2023_hierarchical}.
  \item Stacking Analytic\hyp{}DPM with OCRA further drops FID to 3.26, demonstrating orthogonality \cite{bao_2022_analytic}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Limitations}
OCRA assumes explicit access to $\hat{\varepsilon}$; implicit (ancestral) samplers are not yet supported. Very low noise levels $\tau<10^{-4}$ trigger the gate more often, causing a minor 3\% latency tail when extreme photorealism is required.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We have shown that a persistent one\hyp{}to\hyp{}three\hyp{}degree angular bias between $\hat{\varepsilon}$ and $\varepsilon_{\star}$ is the primary quality bottleneck of ultra\hyp{}low\hyp{}step diffusion sampling. By exploiting the small\hyp{}norm, near\hyp{}collinear and time\hyp{}smooth structure of the residual field we introduced OCRA, a 1.5~M\hyp{}parameter adapter that corrects orientation while keeping the pretrained backbone frozen. OCRA trains in under half an hour, adds negligible runtime cost, and delivers two\hyp{} to five\hyp{}fold speed\hyp{}ups and up to 55\% energy savings without sacrificing sample fidelity. Extensive experiments demonstrate zero\hyp{}shot transfer across backbones and modalities, numerical robustness secured by spectral\hyp{}norm clipping and adaptive gating, and superiority over state\hyp{}of\hyp{}the\hyp{}art timestep and transport correctors. Future work includes coupling OCRA with analytic variance estimators, extending orientation theory to implicit samplers, and exploring benefits for adversarial robustness and solver order adaptivity.
